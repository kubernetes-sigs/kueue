# sohankunkerkar: review profile

In this data, sohankunkerkar comments now and then from 2025-03, becomes a regular
reviewer from 2025-11, and first uses `/approve` on 2026-06-15 (PR #12279). Since then they
approve often (113 `/approve`, 200 `/lgtm`). The review data has 729 threads (539 PRs, 190
issues) and 2,766 of their own entries. 1,894 of those are on other people's threads: 896
inline review comments, 770 conversation comments and 228 review bodies. The other 872 are
replies on their own 176 threads. Their activity grows over time. On others' threads they
wrote 54 entries in 2025-Q4, about 270 per quarter in 2026-Q1 and Q2, 549 in Q3 and 740 in
Q4 (to 11-24). Where their inline comments land on others' PRs: KEPs 39% (350), test code
17% (151), controllers/jobframework/job integrations about 13%, queue/scheduler caches
about 10%, and scheduler, TAS, MultiKueue, webhooks, APIs and docs 1–3% each. DRA is the
main subject. Most of their long threads are DRA KEPs and fixes (#13601, #14130, #10973,
#9742, #10320, #18632). After that come elastic jobs/workload slices, WaitForPodsReady,
partial preemption (#14589), TAS, custom metric labels and MultiKueue.

## What they care about most

Shares are rough: they come from keyword stats over about 1,500 substantive comments, and one
comment can hit several themes. See Method.

1. **Behavior traced through the code: edge cases, what really happens, races, and whether every stuck state has an exit and a message the user can act on** (the biggest share; about 22% of comments are questions, and most of those are "what happens when…").
2. **KEP rigor and readability**: claims about today's code checked against main, scope, interactions, gates, validation, Beta/GA criteria, Alternatives, user stories that still hold, terminology, tables, each point said once, no implementation detail, known gaps written into Risks/Drawbacks, and the KEP kept in sync with the code (39% of their inline comments are on KEPs; prose and structure comments about 7% in 2026-H2).
3. **Tests that actually prove the change**: missing cases (every new branch and each integration sharing the code), gate-off cases, testing through the real entry point with production-shaped inputs, and running the change with a mutation ("still passes without the fix") (17% of inline comments are on test files; test requests about 3%; mutation checks rise in H2).
4. **Feature gates and upgrade or backport safety**: behavior with the gate on and off, crashloops on upgrade, cherry-pick shape (about 8% mention gates; about 3% upgrade or backport).
5. **Release process hygiene**: release notes (they write 32 release-note blocks for authors), CP requests, rebases, `/kind`, PR title and description, `Fixes` vs `Part of` (CP/backport about 11% of entries in H2).
6. **Simplicity and reuse**: drop dead or redundant code, reuse existing helpers, wrappers and patterns (about 7% "drop/remove", about 6% "reuse/already does").
7. **Accurate names and comments**: names that match behavior and local patterns, comments that are not stale or wordy (about 6% naming, about 4% comment wording).
8. **Log verbosity and performance on hot paths** (about 2–4% each; logging mostly in H1, before `logging_policy.md` fixed the levels in 2026-10).

## How they review

- **They verify before they assert.** They often run the code: they revert the fix and check
  that the tests still pass, run a scenario on kind or envtest, read the Prow logs, or trace
  the code to a line. Typical lines are "I tried it.", "I ran #15577 on kind", and "Thanks,
  both cases fail now with their mutation." (#19172). They also own their mistakes in public
  ("Good catch, that one's on me. I asked for it.", #15888, and "Sorry for the false alarm",
  #12258).
- **Tone.** Polite, direct and short. Most comments are questions or soft proposals:
  "I think" (142), "I'd …" (132), "Could you/we" (90), "Can we/you" (62), "Worth …" (47),
  "IIUC" (21). "nit:" appears in 128 comments, and they mark blocking vs non-blocking
  explicitly ("Not a blocker", "non-blocking", "None of them need to block the merge, happy
  for them to land here or in a follow-up.", #14700). Reviews usually open or close with
  "Thanks for working on this!" plus "a few nits but overall LGTM".
- **Concrete fixes.** 94 comments carry a ```suggestion``` block. In KEPs they often rewrite
  whole paragraphs, tables or Graduation Criteria (#14589, #13601, #16535), and once shared
  a gist as a structural example ("this is just an example so don't copy as-is").
- **Before /lgtm or /approve they want:**
  - blocking correctness issues fixed, listed explicitly ("The rest are nits. Happy to approve once those are in.", #14589);
  - a test that fails without the change;
  - green CI ("Will add a `lgtm` once the CI is green.", #9232);
  - a current release note and description;
  - for perf changes, numbers;
  - for KEPs, being added as reviewer/approver in kep.yaml and the Alpha scope settled ("I'm happy with the alpha version here.", #16535).
- **What they let go.** They let nits wait for a follow-up and often file it themselves.
  From 2026-08 they routinely ask `@coderabbitai` to open unassigned `kind/cleanup` issues
  (53 such requests). They accept a smaller scope for Alpha with a Beta criterion, and they
  accept "no e2e" when a unit or integration test covers the logic. They defer to other
  approvers on holds and on approval across companies. They noted that KEPs should get
  review from more than one company (#14104).
- **Process roles.** They do much of the release work: approve cherry-picks (many `/lgtm`,
  `/approve` and `/cherry-pick` on robot PRs), ask authors for manual CPs ("could you create
  CP for 0.19/0.18?"), run `/release-note-edit` (34) and `/kind` relabels, ask for rebases,
  track releases, and nudge stalled PRs ("do you have cycles to push this to the finish
  line?"), reassigning them when needed.
- **On issues.** They triage with root-cause analysis from logs or code, propose the fix
  shape, ask reporters for numbers or repros before designing in-tree (#16885, #18717), and
  push back on feature requests that existing mechanisms already cover (#10343, #17433).

## Author checklist

- [ ] Names match their siblings, the type, local patterns and KEP terms; no new or overloaded terms; log and error messages say exactly what the code did.
- [ ] Every comment, godoc and site docs page the change touches still tells the truth, and docs tell admins where to set it, what is on by default and its limits; comments are one or two lines, with no fix history and no "Alpha"/"implemented" wording.
- [ ] New exported constants, non-obvious functions and API, config or annotation fields have a doc comment (intent, values, meaning of 0/unset); code kept in sync with another place says so at both; tracked follow-ups have `TODO(#issue)`.
- [ ] No dead code, unreachable guards, duplicate helpers, one-caller wrappers, or checks the apiserver or a callee already does.
- [ ] Searched for an existing helper, wrapper or pattern (`utiltesting`, `testingjobs`, existing handlers) before writing one.
- [ ] Logs follow the `logging_policy.md` level table: no V(0) per workload or cycle (per-object decisions at V(3)); "impossible" states visible in default logs (V(1) for one component, V(0) Kueue-wide).
- [ ] Errors are never silently discarded (returned, reported, or logged without `V(n).Error`); invariants hold by construction (no manual unlocks, no nil as a hidden flag).
- [ ] Behavior checked with each relevant feature gate on and off; the gate check is an explicit `features.Enabled` in the outer guard, where siblings or main put it.
- [ ] No new startup or validation failure for a config that loads today: gated on release branches; gated, warned or ACTION REQUIRED on main.
- [ ] Traced all callers, all integrations, MultiKueue workers, restarts and the default config, not only the path you fixed; wrote down what happens to existing objects on gate on/off, restart and downgrade.
- [ ] Every state that holds quota or parks a Workload has an exit (event, timeout, retry); every rejection or stall tells the user the cause.
- [ ] New fields and annotations have validation (bounds, sibling relations, 0/empty); statically invalid input that the webhook doesn't reject is refused with a message the user can act on.
- [ ] Events, metrics and status writes fire only on a real change; metric series are deleted when their object or label goes away and re-reported on recreate, resume and every return path.
- [ ] No new full scans or List calls per reconcile, cycle or candidate; perf claims come with numbers.
- [ ] Reverted the fix locally and confirmed the new test fails; tests go through the production entry point with production-shaped inputs, and cover gate-off, the reverse direction, every new branch and each integration that shares the code.
- [ ] New unit cases are rows in the existing table of the right file and suite, named for what they test; no closures, test-only helpers, constant-per-row fields or default gate settings; existing cases left untouched; integration and e2e tests add no cleanup that `DeleteNamespace` already does.
- [ ] Assertions pin the claim (exact values, UIDs, non-empty checks, `behavioral` timeouts); no debug prints, and no `Gosched`/sleep used as an ordering guarantee.
- [ ] One concern per PR: unrelated rewording, refactors and breaking parts split out, no incidental tweaks the fix doesn't need; design changes have a KEP.
- [ ] KEP: claims about today's behavior checked against main, design not implementation, explicit, narrowest-first Alpha scope and gate, an interactions row per neighbor feature, deferred gaps as Beta criteria and GA filled, Alternatives with reasons, stories and worked examples that still hold, terminology, tables, each point once, exact identifiers, gaps in Risks/Drawbacks, kep.yaml consistent, a POC or draft implementation linked; KEP updated when the implementation departs from it.
- [ ] Release note says what users see, names the gate when the change only applies behind it, has `ACTION REQUIRED:` for upgrade steps, uses `<Area>: Fixed a bug where …`; `/kind` is correct.
- [ ] Title works as the squash commit; description current, with side effects, manual checks and follow-ups; `Part of #N` vs `Fixes #N` is right; commit messages clean, and parts that must merge separately in separate PRs; branch rebased on main.
- [ ] Bug fix: CP plan naming which supported branches the bug affects (and pick dependencies), CP tests compile there, and picks carry a release note.

## Method

- **Data.** All of this reviewer's PR and issue comments in kubernetes-sigs/kueue up to
  2026-11-24 (inline review comments with their diff hunk, review bodies, and
  top-level comments), each read together with the surrounding thread. Bot comments
  are excluded.
- **Analysis.** Theme shares come from keyword statistics over all of the reviewer's
  comments, checked by close reading of threads sampled across every year and area.
  Treat the shares as rough weights: themes overlap and keywords miss paraphrases.
- **Evidence.** Every quote is copied from the comment it links to, and each link was
  resolved through the GitHub API by matching the quote against that reviewer's
  comments on the PR. Quotes from PRs since deleted from GitHub were dropped.
- **Limits.**
  - Theme shares come from keyword matching. They are rough, overlap, and undercount themes stated without keywords (most correctness analysis has no keyword).
  - Long bodies were truncated in my reading copy, so a few nuances inside very long KEP comments may be missed.
  - Bot text quoted inside their entries (Prow, CodeRabbit) slightly inflates some counts.
  - The review data ends 2026-11-24, and Q4 is partial but already the busiest.
  - Their DRA ownership means DRA-specific rules are overrepresented compared with what they would say on other areas.
