# mimowo: review profile

mimowo is a top-level Kueue approver and, in the data, the main release and cherry-pick coordinator. Activity runs from February 2022 to November 2026. The review data holds 10,540 threads (8,678 PRs, 1,861 issues), 1,498 of them their own PRs, and 37,069 of their own entries. 5,244 of those entries are on their own PRs. On other people's PRs there are 11,854 inline comments, and 23,163 entries with real text (not only slash commands). Volume grows every year: 179 entries in 2022, 1,452 in 2023, 5,142 in 2024, 12,125 in 2025 and 18,171 in 2026. They put `/approve` on 6,398 other people's PRs, and only 1,441 of those (23%) also carry their own inline comments. Most approvals land after someone else has done the line-by-line pass. The paths with the most inline comments are `pkg/controller/jobs` (810), `pkg/controller/jobframework` (647), `pkg/controller/admissionchecks` (608, mostly MultiKueue and ProvisioningRequest), `test/integration/singlecluster` (542), `pkg/controller/core` (510), `pkg/cache/scheduler` (493), `site/content` (465), `apis/kueue` (366), `pkg/cache/queue` (266), TAS (`pkg/controller/tas` plus the TAS cache, about 230), and KEPs (about 1,380 inline comments across `keps/`).

## What they care about most

Shares are of inline comments on other people's PRs (n=11,854) and come from keyword matching, so treat them as rough.

1. **Tests: right level, real coverage, no flakes.** About 16% of inline comments mention tests, and flakes come up in 3–4% of all comments since 2025. The top 2026 test asks are concrete: one `ginkgo.It` per scenario instead of `DescribeTable` in integration tests, wrapper builders for fixtures, 100% unit coverage of new logic, and fake clocks. They also want tests that are fast (minimal objects, short durations), deterministic and realistic (the controller under test sets the state), and flake fixes that start from a root cause and leave production code alone.
2. **API, KEP and design alignment.** About 11% are about API, fields or validation, and about 6% about KEPs or design. Much of the KEP review is about structure: what goes in Proposal vs Design Details, graduation criteria, and `kep.yaml` metadata.
3. **Scope: keep the PR focused.** Revert unrelated changes, move extras to a follow-up or a separate PR: 7% of inline comments (follow-up or separate PR) plus about 3% "revert" or "unrelated".
4. **Simplicity and reuse.** Remove what isn't needed (including state that duplicates derivable information), reuse an existing helper, extract a named helper: about 12% combined. Extracting a named helper went from 0.3% in 2023 to 2.4% in 2026.
5. **Feature gates as a safety net for risky changes.** About 6% of 2026 inline comments (4% of all text comments that year), up from under 1% in 2023. Smaller by volume than scope or simplicity, but the ask usually blocks the PR. Bug-fix gates are Beta on main and Alpha on release branches.
6. **Cherry-pickability and PR hygiene.** Release notes, squashing, CP prep. Mostly in top-level comments: 3,566 `/cherrypick` commands, 735 `/release-note-edit` (replaced by `/set-release-note` from late September 2026, 85 uses so far), about 370 comments about release notes. They also relabel `kind/` (106 PRs with `/remove-kind`), because it decides the release-note section and the cherry-pick, and they classify CI failures as related, a known flake or a new flake before retesting.
7. **Errors and logging.** Don't silence errors, pick the log level by policy: about 5% errors and 4% logging. Log-level comments grew to about 3% of inline comments in 2026.
8. **Naming, comments and small nits.** "nit" appears in 6% of inline comments, naming in 3%, code comments in 6%.

## How they review

- **Triage and delegation.** Most entries are process work: 6,612 `/lgtm`, 6,546 `/approve`, 3,566 cherry-pick commands, 1,057 `/ok-to-test`, 715 `/hold`. The most common short comments are "Thank you 👍" (549), "Thanks 👍" (333), "cc @X ptal" (158) and "Leaving lgtm to @X" (34). A typical flow: they ask a domain owner for the first pass ("cc @X would you like to give it a pass?", 671 such delegations, rising to 3.6% of comments in 2026), then add `/approve` themselves. They often combine approve with `/hold` "in case @tenzen-y has some comments" so another approver can weigh in.
- **Question-led design review.** 22–29% of comments contain a question. Typical openers are "IIUC…", "I'm wondering if…", "Let me know if I'm missing something" and "wdyt?" ("wdyt" rose from 4.5% of inline comments in 2023 to 10% in 2026). In an early large review they summarized under "Major comments:" ([PR #475](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/pull/475#pullrequestreview-1219317006) review 2022-12-16). On algorithm PRs they reason about complexity, scale ("TAS is already used in prod at scale") and race windows in event propagation.
- **Severity is labelled.** "nit", "non-blocking nit" and "feel free" mark optional items (about 250 "non-blocking/feel free/up to you"). They let nits slide into follow-ups to unblock merges: "Since the remaining comments are all nit-like, let's merge and address them next week." ([PR #5933](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/pull/5933#issuecomment-3118172242) comment 2025-07-25). They also approve with a hold: "The remaining comments aren't really blocking, but I would like to see them addressed eventually." ([PR #7300](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/pull/7300#issuecomment-3415721583) comment 2025-10-17).
- **Follow-ups are tracked, not dropped.** "open an issue" or "let me open" appears about 206 times. Since mid-2026 they also say "@coderabbitai please open a follow up/cleanup issue…" (69 times, about 250 CodeRabbit mentions in total), often with an assignee.
- **Before /approve they typically want:** comments addressed or resolved; tests at the right level that fail without the fix; flake-prone tests run in a loop; a user-facing release note (often rewritten by them); a feature gate for risky behaviour; a squashed history when interim commits cause `needs-rebase` (the cherry-pick script squashes by itself since #17200); green CI or a classified flake ("known flake", "infra"). For releases they decide which branches get cherry-picks and ask authors to "prepare CP for 0.19".
- **What they usually let go:** small style differences ("I'm ok to keep as is"), optimizations they doubt but that are harmless, partial test coverage for alpha features when there is a ticket for the rest, and cleanups that can move to a follow-up.
- **Tone.** Polite and direct: about 18% of 2026 comments thank the author, often with 👍. Requests are imperative but short ("Extract to helper", "Wrap the code in FG", "nit, revert"). Under load they say so openly and redirect: "Sorry, I'm under water with reviews / approvals." ([PR #15828](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/pull/15828#issuecomment-6083467174) comment 2026-10-09).

## Changes over time

- **2022–2023 (about 1,600 entries).** Detailed line-by-line reviews, as a peer reviewer next to alculquicondor and ahg-g. The highest share of hedged phrasing ("I would suggest" 7.7%, "IIUC" 6.1%), plenty of nits (8% of inline comments) and reverts of unrelated changes. Focus areas: jobframework, the visibility API, integrations, webhooks.
- **2024.** Becomes the approver for TAS, MultiKueue and release management. Cherry-pick commands and `/release-note-edit` appear. KEP review grows (KEPs reach 3.3% of inline comments). Thanks rise to 10%.
- **2025.** Main release and cherry-pick owner. Flakes double to 3.7% of comments. Explicit asks to run tests in a loop and for tests that fail without the fix. Feature gate comments rise to about 3% of comments (4% of inline comments). Release notes get rewritten to a user-impact style with an area prefix.
- **2026 (18k entries, the most of any year).** More delegation (3.6%) and terse imperatives. New recurring asks: extract a named helper (2.4% of inline comments, up from 0.2–0.5%), log levels per the new logging policy (about 3%), FG-guarding at entry points (1.1%), the `featureGates` map pattern in tests, no `DescribeTable` in integration tests (from mid-2026, then written into `writing_tests.md`, with an exception for webhook and CRD validation tests added in November), 100% coverage of new logic, and bug-fix gates as Beta on main. AI enters the reviews: CodeRabbit files follow-up issues for them, they ask authors to disclose AI use and whether a bug was AI-found, and they disclose their own AI-assisted analysis. Nits fall to 5.3% and "I would suggest" to 2.8%, so the style is more directive than in the early years.

## Author checklist

- [ ] Diff contains only changes needed for the fix: no formatting, empty-line or rename churn.
- [ ] Side fixes and cleanups are in separate PRs, mechanical prerequisites in a preparatory PR, and tests that need separate cherry-picks are decoupled.
- [ ] No unused API fields, constants or code paths. No new exports from `jobframework` without a reason.
- [ ] Searched for an existing helper before writing new logic. Nothing is duplicated from another integration.
- [ ] Complex inline blocks are moved into small named helpers, code is flat, and feature-specific code sits in its own file (or its own subpackage when it will keep growing). Workload-keyed maps use `workload.Reference`, membership uses `sets.Set`, and there are no magic numbers. No defensive checks for invariants that validation guarantees, internal contract breaks panic with a message instead of silently defaulting, user input never reaches a panic, and no state that duplicates derivable information. New in-memory state is cleaned up on every delete path, with a test for the leak, and is rebuilt after a restart (handle `Create`, test a manager restart). Transient errors are returned for backoff (no fixed `RequeueAfter` or polling) and permanent ones are not retried, event handlers only filter and enqueue (batched with `constants.UpdatesBatchPeriod`; no API calls, heavy work or fallible calls), and webhooks send no writes. A new feature's state sits in its own type with its lock, optional parameters use variadic options, generic code has no integration-specific `if`s, and sibling integrations and controllers got the same change.
- [ ] Names match behaviour (no `get` prefix on functions that compute or mutate), field and variable names don't repeat their context, and follow local conventions (timeout-constant scale, Kubernetes analogues for config fields).
- [ ] Comments explain the why and match the current code. Obvious comments are removed. New fields, types and exported functions have doc comments, and gated annotations and fields say they are alpha-level and name the gate. Every `TODO` is `TODO(#N)` with a tracking issue.
- [ ] No swallowed errors: return them, otherwise log them. No second log for an error already logged higher up.
- [ ] Log levels follow `logging_policy.md` (anomalies V(0)–V(1), main decisions V(3), details V(4), traces V(5)). No log without a verbosity level. Logs go through the contextual logger, never `klog` functions. No new `context.Background()`/`TODO()`.
- [ ] Risky or possibly disruptive behaviour changes, including fixes to be cherry-picked, sit behind a feature gate that is checked first, and new controllers, indexers and watches are registered only when it is on. Alpha off, Beta on. Bug-fix gates are Beta on main (Alpha when backported), with one `kube_features.go` line for the next release and a name that matches the gate's scope. An off-by-default gate has an experiment PR with it turned on.
- [ ] Patches are sent only when something changed, through strict `clientutil.Patch`/`PatchStatus` (no `Update` of Workloads; loose mode and `WithRetryOnConflict` only in the scheduler), and Event and Condition messages are truncated. KEPs: Proposal is high-level, Graduation Criteria per stage, `kep.yaml` current.
- [ ] API changes match the KEP (update the KEP if not). `make lint-api` passes, and `MinLength`/`MinItems`/`Minimum` are set where empty or zero makes no sense. Validation is strict from the start, new fields are immutable unless a story needs mutability, switches are enum modes rather than bools, and every KEP field has a use case (or Alpha uses a hard-coded default, in-memory state or an annotation). Non-trivial proposals are presented at wg-batch.
- [ ] Renames that affect upgrade are in their own PR, tested on a live cluster, and any admin steps are in an `ACTION REQUIRED:` note. Objects created by the previous version keep working, old cleanup code stays 2 releases, and replaced flags or fields keep working for one release. Behaviour that depends on a real API server is confirmed on a real cluster.
- [ ] A test fails without the fix, and the PR says which assert. A flake fix names the root cause, is minimal, and doesn't change production code or manifests for a test problem.
- [ ] Tests are fast (fewest objects, shortest durations, Job rather than JobSet, per-spec manager and no `ginkgo.Ordered` in singlecluster integration tests) and deterministic (explicit order, no timestamp or sorting tricks), with realistic setups where the controller under test sets the state. Case names describe the scenario and match the bug, `want*` fields come last, and config-changing or Alpha-gated e2e tests sit in the sequential suite.
- [ ] Tests use the cheapest level that proves the behaviour (unit, then integration, then e2e), and unit tests go through `Reconcile`, predicates or `schedule()`, not inner helpers.
- [ ] New scenarios are new cases (new table rows or `It`s) with the existing cases unchanged, and new logic is covered 100% by unit tests (coverage stated). Unit tests are table-driven, use the `featureGates` map, a fake clock (or `synctest`) and real code or `internal/mocks` (no stubs), and assert errors by type or identity, not by message. Integration tests use one `It` per scenario (`DescribeTable` only for webhook and CRD validation tests), wrapper builders, `Eventually` around writes, and cleanup without Go `defer`. Ginkgo tests use `ginkgo.By` steps (not step comments), `test/util/behavioral` helpers and timeout constants, and clean up. Asserts compare sets or objects, not bools.
- [ ] New integration and e2e tests ran in a loop (about 100 times, 20–50 for slow ones; locally or in an experiment PR), and the result is in the PR.
- [ ] The PR title states the area and exact scope (`WIP:` until ready; KEP PRs `KEP-<N>: <title> [NEW]`/`[UPDATE]`). The description explains the user-facing problem and any non-obvious choice, and for a non-trivial fix links the agreed approach or lists the options considered.
- [ ] The release note is `<Area>: <user-visible effect>` inside the `release-note` block, or `NONE`, with `ACTION REQUIRED:` only when admins must act. The description says `Fixes #N` only for full fixes (otherwise `Part of`), commit messages have no `#N`, and for AI-found bugs whether a real workload hit them.
- [ ] The `kind/` label matches the user impact (bug vs feature vs cleanup). CI failures are classified (related, known flake with link, or new flake issue) before a retest. Performance and CI-speed changes carry before/after numbers.
- [ ] Docs link to canonical pages, and samples live in `site/static/examples`. A new page is listed in the Tasks index and cross-linked, task pages start with "Before you begin", and new prose is not translated into zh-CN (mechanical sync fixes only).
- [ ] New dependencies are pinned to tags and maintained by dependabot, and new CI targets come with a test-infra PR.
- [ ] Commits are squashed when asked (cherry-pick, or `needs-rebase` from interim commits), test-only and refactor PRs are ready to be cherry-picked too, and every review thread has a reply, resolved only after the fix is pushed. Replies are your own, with any AI use disclosed there and in the PR description. A human opens the PR, and commits carry no AI co-author trailers.

## Method

- **Data.** All of this reviewer's PR and issue comments in kubernetes-sigs/kueue up to
  2026-11-24 (inline review comments with their diff hunk, review bodies, and
  top-level comments), each read together with the surrounding thread. Bot comments
  are excluded.
- **Analysis.** Theme shares come from keyword statistics over all of the reviewer's
  comments, checked by close reading of threads sampled across every year and area.
  Treat the shares as rough weights: themes overlap and keywords miss paraphrases.
- **Evidence.** Every quote is copied from the comment it links to, and each link was
  resolved through the GitHub API by matching the quote against that reviewer's
  comments on the PR. Quotes from PRs since deleted from GitHub were dropped.
- **Limits.** Theme shares come from keyword regex and overlap (one comment can match several themes). Some text from embedded issue or bot entries may have leaked past the filters. Shares measure how often a theme is mentioned, not how much it weighs when a PR is blocked. Most own-PR activity was excluded. The logging policy itself (its exact levels) is referenced but not quoted in full in the review data.
